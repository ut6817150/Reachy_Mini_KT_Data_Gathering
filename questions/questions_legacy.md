# Interview Questions

Add exactly ten questions using the following heading format:

## Question 1

A panel of psychologists interviewed and administered personality tests to 30 engineers and 70 lawyers, all successful in their respective fields. Brief descriptions were written from this information. Here is one, chosen at random from the 100 available descriptions. Adam is a 30 year old man. He is married with no children. A man of high ability and high motivation, he promises to be quite successful in his field. He is well liked by his colleagues. What is the probability that Adam is one of the 30 engineers rather than one of the 70 lawyers? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 2

Suppose a tennis player reaches the Wimbledon final. He has to win 3 out of 5 sets to win the final. Consider two possible events. The first is that the player will win the first set. The second is that the player will win the first set but lose the match. Is one of these events more likely than the other, or are they equally likely? Explain your reasoning.

## Question 3

A cancer test is administered to all the residents in a large city. A positive result is indicative of cancer and a negative result of no cancer. Now compare two probabilities. The first is the probability that a person has in fact cancer, supposing that they got a positive result. The second is the probability of a positive test, supposing that the person has cancer. Is one of these more likely than the other, or are they equally likely? Explain your reasoning.

## Question 4

Two black and two white marbles are put in an urn. We pick a marble from the urn. Then, without putting it back into the urn, we pick a second marble at random. If the second marble is white, what is the probability that the first marble was white? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 5

According to a recent survey, 91 percent of the population in a city do lie, and 36 percent of those lie about important matters. If we pick a person at random from this city, what is the probability that the person lies about important matters? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 6

60 percent of the population in a city are men, 40 percent women. 50 percent of the men and 25 percent of the women smoke. We select a person from the city at random. What is the probability that this person is a smoker? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 7

A test detects a disease whose prevalence is 1 in 1000. Whenever a person has the disease, the test is positive. However, the test also has a false positive rate of 5 percent, meaning that 5 percent of healthy people also test positive. A person is selected at random, given the test, and the result is positive. What is the chance that this person actually has the disease? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 8

Two machines M1 and M2 produce balls. Machine M1 produces 40 percent and M2 60 percent of balls. 5 percent of the balls produced by M1 and 1 percent of those produced by M2 are defective. We take a ball at random and it is defective. What is the probability that the ball was produced by machine M1? Explain how you arrived at your answer.

## Question 9

A witness sees a crime involving a taxi in a city. The witness says that the taxi is blue. It is known from previous research that witnesses are correct 80 percent of the time when making such statements. The police also know that 15 percent of the taxis in the city are blue, the other 85 percent being green. What is the probability that a blue taxi was involved in the crime?

## Question 10

The probability of breast cancer is 1 percent for a woman at age forty who participates in routine screening. If a woman has breast cancer, the probability is 80 percent that she will get a positive mammography. If a woman does not have breast cancer, the probability is 9.6 percent that she will also get a positive mammography. A woman in this age group had a positive mammography in a routine screening. What is the probability that she actually has breast cancer?
